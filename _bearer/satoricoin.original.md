## Background

The original Satori Coin was the first physical bitcoin made in Japan. Yoshi Kan of the import
company Raimu Inc. [announced it in March 2016](https://www.ccn.com/satori-coin-a-physical-bitcoin-in-japan/).
The coin looks like a poker chip and holds 0.001 BTC. According to
[Pascal Bergeron's collector guide](https://pascal-bergeron.com/en/posts/satori-coin-guide/),
production stopped in 2018 due to regulatory pressure, after 50,500 units. We found no exact end
date.

The brand returned in 2025 under Satori Club Pte Ltd in Singapore, with Motonori Kan as key principal,
with the {% include walletLink.html wallet='bearer/satoricoin.gi' verdict='true' %} and the
{% include walletLink.html wallet='bearer/satoricoin.chi' verdict='true' %}. Satori's site still
offers a balance lookup and redeem instructions for the original coins.

## Analysis

Each coin carries a serial number tied to an address, and the private key is printed as a QR code
under a tamper-evident hologram. To [redeem](https://satoricoin.com/redeem/original), the owner peels
the hologram and sweeps the key with a wallet.

The key was generated by the provider, and the coins shipped loaded. Satori itself
[contrasts](https://satoricoin.com/products/gi) the newer multisig model with this one:

> Unlike the original Satori Coin, you no longer need to trust that we can access your private keys.

The coin is {% include verdictBadge.html verdict="prefilled" type='short' %}. Buyers on the secondary
market should check the hologram and the balance before paying a premium for a loaded coin.
