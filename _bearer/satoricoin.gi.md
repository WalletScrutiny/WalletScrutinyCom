## Background

Satori Coin Gi is the flagship of the relaunched Satori Coin line by Satori Club Pte Ltd in
Singapore. The first Satori Coins were sold in Japan from 2016
({% include walletLink.html wallet='bearer/satoricoin.original' verdict='true' %}). The Gi went on
sale on [September 25, 2025](https://news.bitcoin.com/physical-bitcoin-collectibles-satori-coins-to-return-with-premium-gi-model/),
with a mintage of 3,000. It holds 0.01 BTC in an aluminium and glass body that screws apart, sealed
with tamper-evident holograms. An NFC tag carries a URL for the serial lookup and the public address.
The cheaper {% include walletLink.html wallet='bearer/satoricoin.chi' verdict='true' %} uses a
single key instead.

Payment is in bitcoin only. Since [July 7, 2026](https://satoricoin.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/43352833692953-Is-KYC-know-your-customer-necessary)
the shop no longer asks for KYC.

## Analysis

The coin is funded to a 2-of-2 multisig address. From the
[product page](https://satoricoin.com/products/gi):

> Your Satori Coin Gi uses a 2-of-2 multisig address. You know both keys, but we only know one (the
> one inside). The second private key is generated by you, client-side, and is never transmitted to
> us.

Their [support article on resale](https://satoricoin.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/52726025967001-How-can-a-secondary-market-of-Satori-Coin-Gi-work)
spells out who knows what:

> **Key #1** is printed inside the coin itself, and it is known to whoever owns and unseals the coin.
> It is also known to us temporarily during manufacturing. Keys are destroyed after successful
> delivery.
>
> **Key #2** is generated by the purchaser client-side, and it is printed inside the Redeem Kit.

The buyer's payment goes to a "Proxy Wallet Address" whose key is also in the Redeem Kit, and from
there to the coin's multisig address. By Satori's account, the provider never holds the loaded funds
alone. During
checkout the buyer moves the mouse to add entropy and downloads the Redeem Kit as a PDF to print.
If the coin gets lost in the mail, the key under its seal is worthless without the Redeem Kit.

That is a real improvement over the original coins and similar to the two-factor key generation of
{% include walletLink.html wallet='bearer/balletcryptoreal' verdict='true' %}. Two caveats remain:

- Key #2 is generated by JavaScript that satoricoin.com serves during checkout, and we found no
  published source for it. A provider that wanted both keys would only have to change that script.
  We could not inspect the checkout itself as it requires an account.
- After a resale, the first owner still holds Key #2, and Satori held Key #1. The new owner relies
  on the hologram seal and on Satori having deleted Key #1.

To redeem, the owner opens the coin and combines Key #1 with the Redeem Kit. Satori's
[redeem page](https://satoricoin.com/redeem/gi) does this in the browser, and they also describe
how to [redeem with coinb.in offline](https://satoricoin.zendesk.com/hc/en-us/articles/50984494101401-How-can-I-redeem-my-Satori-Coin-Gi-on-my-own).
Either way both private keys end up on a general-purpose computer, which earns the coin our
{% include verdictBadge.html verdict="sealed-plainkey" type='short' %} verdict.

**Note:** The GitHub repository `Ihaytoo/satori` calls itself "The Official Repository for Satori
Coin", but it contains a claim portal for a Solana token called $SATORI. satoricoin.com does not link
to it. Satori Coin is sold for bitcoin only.
