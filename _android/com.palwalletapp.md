## App Description

PalWallet is a cryptocurrency wallet application that claims to be a "self-custody" platform supporting over 40 blockchains and 16,000 crypto assets. The app offers features including cryptocurrency swaps, staking with up to 20% APY, interest-earning accounts up to 17% APY, and merchant payment integration through PalMerchant. The platform advertises proprietary security technology called "PalShield" using encryption algorithms and blockchain-based insurance policies via smart contracts. According to the App Store listing, the developer states no data is collected from users, though the app provides wallet recovery through what they describe as a "unique user signature 5FA" system.

## Analysis

We [tested](https://x.com/BitcoinWalletz/status/1993562463825281524) the app and documented the setup process. 

The wallet setup included:
1. 5-digit passcode
2. Advanced encryption passphrase (password)
3. Seed phrase backup options:
   - Google Drive backup
   - Manual backup (12-word seed phrase provided)

**Critical Issue:** We attempted to import the 12-word seed phrase into Electrum to verify self-custody claims. The Bitcoin address generated by PalWallet (`bc1qhyj4r56dv6fj04cgaxvrgk4maah6gsagx6fd9y`) [did not match](https://x.com/BitcoinWalletz/status/1993564027235254461) any addresses derived from the seed phrase in Electrum. This indicates either:
- PalWallet uses a proprietary/non-standard derivation method, making recovery impossible without their app
- The seed phrase does not actually control the private keys
- The implementation is broken

A truly self-custodial wallet must allow users to recover funds using standard BIP39-compatible wallets. **This app fails that test and cannot be verified as self-custodial**.

This is particularly concerning since their [terms of service](https://palwallet.com/terms-of-service/) state: 

> Upon termination, the user’s access to the services will be disabled, and any data retained will be handled in accordance with our Privacy Policy and applicable law.

[PalShield](https://palwallet.com/palshield/) uses Multi-Party Computation (MPC) to split private keys into pieces stored across both the user's device and PalWallet's servers, combined with a "5FA" system requiring device ownership, password, behavioral metrics, biometric data (face and voice), and server verification. This architecture means users cannot recover funds using only their seed phrase, as the recovery process requires active participation from PalWallet's servers to authenticate the five factors and reconstruct the key fragments. Despite marketing claims of "self-custody", this server-dependent design makes PalWallet effectively custodial, as users lose access to their funds if the company's infrastructure becomes unavailable.
